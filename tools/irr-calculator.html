How this is calculated

Each row is one calendar month, from the 1st of the purchase month to the 1st of the sale month. The IRR is XIRR over the Net CF column against those dates (actual/365, the Excel convention).

  • Interest = loan × (cash rate + margin + buffer) ÷ 12. Interest-only — no principal is amortised.
  • Property value compounds geometrically from purchase price to sold price across the hold period, and snaps to the sold price on the final row.
  • Running costs and rent are struck off that month's property value (rent uses the purchase/sold midpoint instead when the rent basis is Flat).
  • Net CF = holding cashflow, plus the deposit in month 1, plus (sold price − loan + selling costs) in the final month.
  • Negative gearing, when on, refunds the accumulated FY loss × marginal tax rate each June and at sale. The FY accumulator resets in July.
  • Hold period uses Excel's YEARFRAC basis 0 (US 30/360).

Columns that would hold the same value on every row are stated once above the table rather than repeated: the loan never changes, and neither does the cash rate on the Normalised basis. Accum loss (FY) and NG refund appear only when negative gearing is on, and Cash rate only on the Actual basis. Export CSV always writes every column, including the loan and cumulative interest, so it still ties column-for-column to the workbook.

Settlement-accurate timing — this is where the tool departs from the workbook. The sheet dates every cashflow to the 1st of its month and charges a full month of interest, rent and running costs at both ends of the hold. That books the sale up to 30 days early and charges roughly one month of costs too many. Here the deposit sits on the purchase date, the sale on the settlement date, and the first and last months accrue pro rata by day. Middle months are unaffected.

Across the 313 properties in the workbook it moves IRR by a median of 11 bps, but up to 167 bps — the effect is concentrated in short holds, where a part month is a larger slice of the term. Median shift is 55 bps under three years against 8 bps beyond six.

How the sold check works

The /exec address of the Apps Script behind the sheet, not the sheet's own link. Making one: sheet-endpoint.gs, about three minutes. Set it once and it is shared with everyone who can sign in, so no one else has to. It is never written into this page, and nothing goes anywhere but your own sheet.

1 · RP Data — the first run, then the weekly catch-up

One button checks every held property in the book, whatever capital it is in. For each one it types the address into RP Data's own search box, opens the property, and reads the last sale and the listing status. A last sale newer than your purchase is the signal — it has changed hands since you bought it.

It runs in your browser on your RP Data sign-in, about eight seconds a property, and writes each row to the sheet as it finds it. It resumes: stop whenever you like and click it again later, and it carries on from where it stopped rather than starting the book again.

Start with this one. On a book nothing has been checked in, it is the one that sweeps the lot — every held property, at about eight seconds each, so the first run is a long sitting. Stop and come back as often as you like; it picks up where it left off.

After that first sweep it settles into a catch-up, and only asks about what the sheet has no answer for — properties you have just added, and the ones RP Data could not resolve. Anything it has already answered is left alone, and the unresolved ones come round again each Monday. From then on it is a short run.

  1. Show the bookmarks bar if it is hidden — Ctrl+Shift+B.
  2. Drag the button below onto it. Dragging, not clicking — clicking it here does nothing, this page is not RP Data.
  3. Open rpp.corelogic.com.au and sign in.
  4. Click the bookmark. Leave that tab in front — Chrome freezes background tabs and the run stalls.
  5. Watch it in the sheet, or press F12 to watch the console. To stop: stopCheck = true in the console, or just close the tab. Everything found so far is already saved.
  6. Come back here and press Load sold check records.

2 · RP Data — the monthly refresh

"Still held" goes stale. This one asks about every held property again, whatever it said last time, so a sale that happened since the last check is found. It skips only what your register already has a sale for, and whatever has already been checked this calendar month — which is also how it resumes. It starts the book over on the 1st.

Not the one to start with: on a book that has never been checked this does the same long sweep as the first bookmark, with none of its catching-up afterwards. Use it once the book is swept, to keep it fresh.

🔄 RP Data monthly Drag me to your bookmarks bar

This is the long one — the whole book at about eight seconds a property — so expect to leave the tab in front for a while, and stop it with stopCheck = true whenever you like. The two bookmarks keep separate memories, so running one never confuses the other.

3 · RP Data — the property type sweep

A one-off, and a different job from the other two. It reads what kind of property each one is — house, unit or development site — and writes nothing else.

It is the only one that opens the pages of properties you have already sold. The other two drop those before they start, and a sold property is exactly what a growth comparison is built from, so without this they never get a type at all.

It skips anything whose Property type cell is already filled — the column is its memory, so it resumes by itself and can be run from any machine. Sold properties go first, so an interrupted sweep has done the half that matters. To ask about one again, clear its cell.

🏠 RP Data types Drag me to your bookmarks bar

It never writes a sale date, a price or a verdict, and it leaves Checked at alone — so running it cannot bring forward or cancel the next sold check. Stop it any time with stopCheck = true.

4 · What it tells you

  1. Sold since purchase. The last sale on record is newer than your purchase date, so it has changed hands. These are the ones that come through to the list to be marked on.
  2. On the market. For Sale, Under Offer, Under Contract, Auction, For Rent or Withdrawn, with the date it was last advertised. A listing is not a sale, so none of it is ever written onto a property — it is counted separately at the top of the tab.
  3. Needs a second look. No suggestion came back, the page did not open, or it opened with no sale on record. The sheet says which. The weekly pass offers them again each Monday — or run rpAskAgain = true in the console to go at them now.

What this reads

A list of properties as they were bought, or a list of sales. Which one is worked out from the columns the file names — a sales export names a sale price and a sale date and nothing about what the place cost to buy. The preview says which it read it as before anything is written.

Headed columns are read by name — Property or Address, Suburb, State, Postcode, Purchase Price, Purchase Date, Market, Notes — in any order. Without a header the order above is assumed; a leading row-number column is skipped. A single column of addresses is read as properties with nothing known about them yet. Dates may be Excel serials (42565), 14/07/2016, 2016-07-14 or 14 Jul 2016. Owner, Vendor, Purchaser and Agent columns are names of people and are never read.
